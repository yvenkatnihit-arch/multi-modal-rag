## 2. Public Health Implications

The "Moderate" risk classification, combined with the specific toxicology of *Dispholidus typus*, necessitates a sober assessment of public health risks for Johannesburg residents and wildlife practitioners.

**Clinical Risk Profile:**
The Boomslang possesses potent hemotoxic venom causing defibrination and coagulopathy. Unlike cytotoxic viperids common in the region, Boomslang envenomation symptoms may be delayed by several hours to days, often leading to underestimation of severity in initial clinical assessments. Fatalities, while rare due to the species' shy demeanor, are documented and occur primarily when snakes are handled or cornered.

**Resident and Practitioner Risks:**
*   **Arboreal Interface:** As an arboreal species, the Boomslang utilizes roof spaces, gutters, and fruit trees. Residents pruning trees or cleaning gutters during the peak Highveld summer activity season (December) are at elevated risk of surprising a specimen.
*   **Misidentification:** Residents frequently confuse Boomslangs with harmless green snakes (*Philothamnus* spp.). This can lead to dangerous complacency regarding venomous specimens or the unnecessary killing of non-venomous ecologically beneficial species.
*   **Medical Response:** Standard pressure immobilization bandages are generally contraindicated for hemotoxic envenomation due to potential local tissue complications; the focus must be on rapid transport to a trauma facility. Medical staff should be briefed on the delayed onset of symptoms to prevent premature discharge of bite victims.
