#### 3. Public Health Implications
Although *Python regius* is non-venomous, the presence of a constrictor species in a high-density urban environment necessitates specific risk communication.

*   **Medical Risk:** Negligible for adults. *P. regius* typically reaches 1.0–1.5 meters. Constriction poses a theoretical risk to small domestic animals and infants, but presents minimal physical threat to adult humans.
*   **Psychological Impact:** High. Ophidiophobia is prevalent. Unverified sightings often generate disproportionate community anxiety, potentially leading to unauthorized killing of snakes or panic-induced injuries.
*   **Misidentification Risk:** Residents often lack the taxonomic literacy to distinguish between exotic *P. regius* and protected native pythons (e.g., *Python natalensis*). This may lead to collateral mortality of indigenous species or unsafe handling attempts.
*   **Zoonotic Concern:** Captive-bred specimens may carry pathogens (e.g., *Salmonella* spp.) transmissible to humans, necessitating strict hygiene protocols during handling.
