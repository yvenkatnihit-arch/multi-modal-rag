## 2. Public Health Implications

Although *Psammophis orientalis* is non-venomous, the assessed displacement dynamics present tangible public health and safety risks for Mombasa residents and wildlife practitioners.

*   **Conflict Escalation & Injury:** The primary risk is defensive aggression rather than envenomation. When cornered in domestic spaces, this species may bite, causing minor injuries susceptible to secondary infections or triggering severe anxiety responses in residents.
*   **Misidentification & Retaliation:** High human density combined with a Habitat Loss Index of 0.62 creates a high probability of lethal retaliation. Residents frequently confuse *P. orientalis* with venomous sympatric species (e.g., *Dendroaspis* spp.), leading to unnecessary killing of ecologically beneficial pest controllers.
*   **Ecosystem Service Disruption:** Displacement or eradication of this predator disrupts local rodent and insect population control, potentially increasing the risk of vector-borne diseases in adjacent residential zones.
*   **Practitioner Safety:** The provided Risk Factors (3.91, 1.71) suggest a heightened probability of encounter and bite incidents during handling. Wildlife removal teams face elevated exposure risks, necessitating strict PPE protocols to prevent injury and ectoparasite transfer.
