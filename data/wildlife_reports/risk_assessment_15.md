### Public Health Implications

Although *B. capensis* is non-venomous and poses no envenomation risk, this assessment carries important public health implications:

**Direct Health Risk:** Negligible for envenomation. However, bite wounds can lead to secondary bacterial infections requiring standard wound care protocols and tetanus prophylaxis.

**Psychological and Behavioral Risk:** The primary concern is psychological distress and panic-induced injury. Residents unfamiliar with herpetofauna may mistake this species for venomous snakes (e.g., *Bitis* or *Dendroaspis* species), leading to dangerous retaliatory attempts that increase injury risk to both humans and snakes.

**Ecological Indicator Value:** Non-venomous species respond to the same displacement drivers as venomous ones. Increased non-venomous urban sightings frequently precede or accompany increases in venomous species encounters as the broader snake community is displaced from deteriorating natural habitat.

**National Context:** Rwanda reports approximately 800 snakebite-related deaths annually according to WHO estimates, placing it among the highest-burden countries for snakebite envenomation in sub-Saharan Africa. This underscores the importance of displacement risk monitoring in rapidly urbanizing centres like Kigali.

**Healthcare Access:** The nearest identified hospital facility is approximately 14.4 km from this sighting location, representing relatively accessible emergency care.

### Recommended Conservation and Emergency Response Actions

**Conservation Actions:**
- **Habitat Corridor Protection:** Prioritize preservation of riparian zones and forest fragments connecting this observation coordinate to larger green spaces, reducing the need for snakes to traverse open urban areas.
- **Urban Planning Integration:** Incorporate herpetofauna sensitivity into Kigali's master planning. Enforce buffer zones of 10–20 meters around remaining forest fragments to minimize edge effects and displacement pressure.
- **Population Monitoring:** Increase citizen science reporting to improve the Sighting Density metric. The current low value (0.4) indicates a significant knowledge gap that limits precision in future USDRI calculations.

**Emergency Response Protocols:**
- **Public Education:** Deploy targeted information in peri-urban zones identifying *B. capensis* as non-venomous and beneficial for rodent control. Visual aids should distinguish it from local venomous species to reduce panic killing.
- **Safe Removal Procedures:** Establish localized response teams trained in non-lethal snake removal, prioritizing relocation to suitable nearby habitat fragments.
- **Clinical Guidelines:** Distribute protocols to local clinics confirming the non-venomous status of *B. capensis* to prevent unnecessary antivenom administration, focusing instead on wound cleaning and tetanus prophylaxis.
- **Resident Guidance:** Encourage wearing closed footwear outdoors, keeping yards clear of debris, woodpiles, and dense low vegetation that provide snake shelter, and familiarising households with the location and operating hours of the nearest antivenom-capable facility.
