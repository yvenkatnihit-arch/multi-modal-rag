## 3. Public Health Implications

**Taxonomic Clarification:**
*Congolacerta vauereselli* is a non-venomous lacertid lizard. Despite the USDRI framework's nomenclature, this species poses **zero envenomation risk** to residents. Public health communications must explicitly distinguish this species from medically significant sympatric snakes (e.g., *Bitis arietans*, *Naja mossambica*) to prevent unnecessary panic and inappropriate emergency responses.

**Implications for Residents:**
- **Nuisance vs. Danger:** Interactions are limited to visual sightings in gardens or peripheral green spaces. The species is insectivorous and provides pest control benefits.
- **Misidentification Risk:** The primary concern is misidentification as a juvenile snake, leading to unnecessary injury to the animal or undue resident stress. Education should focus on morphological distinctions (limbs, ear openings, eyelids).
- **Zoonotic Potential:** Standard hygiene protocols apply—Salmonella transmission risk via handling is negligible but present; handwashing is sufficient mitigation.

**Implications for Practitioners:**
- **Triage Protocols:** Emergency calls should be classified as "Wildlife Presence" rather than "Venomous Snake Removal."
- **Clinical Resource Allocation:** Antivenom resources should not be allocated for encounters with this species. Clarification prevents misallocation of critical medical supplies.

## 4. Action Plan: Conservation and Emergency Response

Given the **Low** risk classification, strategies prioritize coexistence and habitat connectivity over removal or intensive intervention.

**Conservation Strategies:**
1. **Habitat Corridor Maintenance:** Preserve rocky outcrops and vegetated buffer zones within Kigali's hilly landscape. These features serve as thermal refugia and movement corridors amidst urban fragmentation.
2. **Urban Planning Integration:** Incorporate *C. vauereselli* presence data into Kigali's master plan. Mandate retention of native vegetation patches in new developments to maintain insect prey bases.
3. **Citizen Science Monitoring:** Leverage the high sighting density by training residents to log occurrences via mobile platforms, enhancing data resolution for future USDRI iterations.

**Emergency Response Protocols:**
1. **Verification:** Responders must confirm species identity. Confirmation of *C. vauereselli* downgrades response from "Hazmat/Venomous" to "General Wildlife."
2. **Relocation vs. Removal:** Lethal control is contraindicated. If inside a dwelling, capture and release within the same property boundary or within 500 meters into suitable vegetated cover.
3. **Public Communication:** Deploy standardized messaging: "This is a harmless, non-venomous lizard. It does not require removal unless inside living spaces."
4. **Injury Management:** In the unlikely event of a defensive bite, standard first aid for minor puncture wounds applies. No antivenom is required; monitor for secondary infection only.
