## 3. Public Health and Safety Implications
For Johannesburg residents and wildlife practitioners, the risk profile associated with *Hemidactylus mabouia* differs fundamentally from venomous Ophidians.

*   **Venomous Risk:** Negligible. *H. mabouia* is non-venomous and poses no direct envenomation threat to humans or domestic animals.
*   **Zoonotic Potential:** Low. As with all reptiles, there is a minor risk of Salmonella transmission via handling or contact with feces; standard hygiene protocols are sufficient mitigation.
*   **Ecological Impact:** The primary consideration is indirect. High densities of this invasive species may indicate competitive pressure on indigenous gecko species (e.g., *Afroedura* spp. or *Lygodactylus* spp.). Residents should be advised that while this species is harmless, its presence indicates an altered ecosystem.
*   **Ecological Benefit:** The species provides significant pest control services, reducing populations of mosquitoes and moths. Removal is generally discouraged unless the animal is inside critical sterile environments (e.g., hospitals).
