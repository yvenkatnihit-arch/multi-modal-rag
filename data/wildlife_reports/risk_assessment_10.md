*   **Urban Expansion Pressure (Metric: 5.0 | Sub-score: 16.0):** This weighted impact reflects the irreversible nature of infrastructure development in hilly landscapes, which limits dispersal corridors. The high derived sub-score indicates steady encroachment that systematically eliminates buffer zones between human settlement and remaining natural habitat.

*   **Urban Proximity Score (Metric: 20.0 | Sub-score: 14.37):** This metric highlights the immediate adjacency of remnant habitat to built environments. High proximity correlates directly with accidental displacement during construction and increased mortality from domestic predators or vehicular traffic.

## 3. Public Health Implications
A critical discrepancy exists between the USDRI framework nomenclature and the biological reality of the subject species. Although the index is designated for "Urban Snake" displacement, *Trioceros ellioti* (Elliot's Chameleon) is a **non-venomous squamate reptile**, not a snake.

*   **Venom Status:** *T. ellioti* poses no envenomation risk to residents, pets, or practitioners. There is no venom apparatus.
*   **Misidentification Risk:** The primary public health implication is behavioral. Residents unfamiliar with local herpetofauna may misidentify *T. ellioti* as a venomous snake (e.g., *Bitis* or *Naja* species) due to the "snake" classification in legacy data sources. This often leads to preemptive killing of the animal—posing no health threat to humans but significant conservation detriment to the species.
*   **Zoonotic Potential:** *T. ellioti* presents negligible direct public health risk and is not a known vector for zoonotic pathogens relevant to the urban context. Standard hygiene protocols apply during handling.
*   **Practitioner Guidance:** Emergency responders and public health officials must update local protocols to reflect that this species requires relocation, not extermination.

## 4. Action Plan
Given the **Moderate** risk classification with a **High Trajectory**, the following strategies are recommended:

### Conservation Strategies
*   **Arboreal Corridor Establishment:** Prioritize the protection and planting of native vegetation strips along ridgelines and property boundaries connecting fragmented forest patches. *T. ellioti* relies on canopy cover; ground-level corridors are insufficient for this scansorial species.
*   **Green Infrastructure Integration:** Mandate the inclusion of vertical greenery (green walls, mature tree retention) in new urban development plans within the identified coordinate cluster (Lat: -1.2974, Lon: 29.9325). This provides microhabitat refuges within the built environment.
*   **Fragment Management:** Implement strict controls on development within the immediate proximity score zone. Require environmental impact assessments that specifically account for arboreal reptile displacement before approving new construction in hilly sectors.
*   **Data Correction:** Submit correction records to GBIF and local databases to rectify the taxonomic error labeling *T. ellioti* as a snake, ensuring precise risk modeling.

### Emergency Response & Community Engagement
*   **Relocation Protocol:** Establish a standardized safe-handling and relocation protocol for local authorities. Specimens found in residential areas should be captured by trained personnel and released into designated green zones, not destroyed.
*   **Public Education Campaign:** Deploy targeted informational materials in the Kigali sector clarifying the non-venomous nature of *T. ellioti*. Materials should include visual identification guides to distinguish chameleons from venomous snakes, emphasizing the species' beneficial role in insect control.
*   **Monitoring Program:** Implement citizen science monitoring to track sighting density changes relative to urban expansion phases. A sharp increase in density without corresponding habitat expansion should trigger a reclassification to "High Risk."
