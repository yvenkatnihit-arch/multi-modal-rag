## 3. Public Health Implications

The displacement of *Dromophis praeornatus* presents specific socio-ecological challenges distinct from venomous species management.

*   **Medical Risk Profile:** As a non-venomous colubrid, the envenomation risk to Kumasi residents is negligible. The primary medical concerns are trauma from defensive strikes and secondary bacterial infections (e.g., *Salmonella*) if bites are untreated.
*   **Psychosocial Impact:** Public anxiety remains high due to potential misidentification of *D. praeornatus* as venomous species. This often leads to unnecessary killing of specimens, posing a conservation risk and increasing injury potential during amateur removal attempts.
*   **Ecological Service Disruption:** *D. praeornatus* is ophiophagous and rodentivorous. Displacement or population collapse of this species may inadvertently reduce natural predation on urban rodent populations, potentially elevating long-term risks associated with rodent-borne diseases such as Lassa fever.

## 4. Action Plan

Given the Moderate risk classification, strategies focus on habitat connectivity maintenance and community education to prevent risk escalation.

### Conservation Strategies
1.  **Presence Verification Survey:** Conduct immediate field surveys (Visual Encounter Surveys and cover board monitoring) to confirm current population viability, updating the 1975 baseline data.
2.  **Habitat Corridor Preservation:** Identify and protect green corridors connecting the assessment coordinates to larger forest fragments. Prevent further fragmentation beyond the current 0.21 loss score by engaging local landowners regarding the ecological service of rodent control.
3.  **Land-Use Zoning:** Advocate for municipal zoning that restricts high-density housing in areas with Expansion Pressure scores >7.0, promoting agroforestry buffers instead.

### Emergency Response Protocols
1.  **Verification and Relocation:** Response teams must verify species identity before removal. *D. praeornatus* should not be euthanized. If relocation is required, move individuals ≥2 km from the capture site to suitable habitat patches to prevent homing.
2.  **Community Education:** Deploy targeted outreach in the peri-urban zone, distributing identification guides distinguishing *D. praeornatus* from sympatric venomous species (e.g., *Bitis* or *Naja* spp.) and establishing safe reporting channels.
3.  **Medical Preparedness:** Local clinics should maintain standard protocols for bite wound cleaning and tetanus prophylaxis to manage secondary infection risks.

### Monitoring and Review
*   **Re-assessment Interval:** 24 months.
*   **Trigger for Escalation:** If Urban Expansion Pressure exceeds 8.5/10 or contemporary sighting density drops below 10.0, the risk level should be recalibrated to **High**, necessitating urgent habitat protection measures.
