## 2. Public Health Implications

Despite the "Low" displacement risk classification, the public health risk associated with *Atheris nitschei* remains **High** due to the species' venom profile and the density of human population in Kigali. Displacement risk refers to ecological stability, not medical safety.

*   **Envenomation Profile:** *A. nitschei* possesses hemotoxic and cytotoxic venom. Bites typically result in severe local swelling, necrosis, and coagulopathy. While mortality is lower than seen with *Bitis* or *Naja* species, morbidity regarding tissue loss and long-term disability is significant.
*   **Clinical Preparedness:** Medical facilities in the Kigali vicinity must maintain awareness of this species' presence. Specific antivenom for *Atheris* species is not always stocked in standard Rwandan medical inventories, which often prioritize polyvalent antivenoms effective against *Bitis arietans* or *Naja melanoleuca*. Clinicians should prepare for supportive care protocols (hematological monitoring, wound management) in the event of envenomation where species-specific antivenom is unavailable.
*   **Community Precautions:** Residents in the fragmented forest interface must be advised against modifying vegetation structure near residential perimeters. Removal of shrubbery and lower canopy branches forces arboreal vipers to ground level, increasing encounter rates. Public education should emphasize visual identification and avoidance rather than attempted capture.
*   **Herpetological Safety:** Practitioners responding to sightings must utilize full personal protective equipment (PPE). Given the Trend Index suggests a stable population, handling protocols should prioritize non-lethal relocation only when absolutely necessary, minimizing stress-induced defensive strikes.
